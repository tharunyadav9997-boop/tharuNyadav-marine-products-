About Our Project

Marine Products and Community Development

Project Overview

This Community Service Project focuses on understanding marine products, their availability, processing techniques and their economic importance to coastal communities.


Project Objectives

  • Identify different marine resources.
  • Study aquaculture and fish farming.
  • Understand pond maintenance.
  • Learn fish disease identification.
  • Explore processing and preservation.
  • Promote sustainable aquaculture.

Mini Project

Sustainable Aquaculture Practices for Marine Products, including approaches such as Recirculating Aquaculture Systems (RAS) and Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture (IMTA).

Our Weekly Activities

Six weeks of learning and practical observations

01

Week 1: Introduction to Aqua Farming

During the first week, we learned about the basic concepts of aquaculture and its importance in the production of fish, prawns and other aquatic organisms. We studied how aquatic organisms are cultivated in controlled water environments for food production and commercial purposes.

Topics Covered:

  • Introduction to aquaculture and its meaning.
  • Difference between freshwater and marine aquaculture.
  • Types of fish farming systems and their importance.
  • Commonly cultured species such as Rohu, Catla, Mrigal, Tilapia, shrimp and freshwater prawns.
  • Importance of aquaculture in food security and employment.
  • Basic requirements for starting a fish farming unit.

Practical Learning:

We observed different types of fish farming systems and learned about fish seed, fingerlings, water availability and suitable farming conditions. We also understood how aquaculture supports rural livelihoods and the local economy.

Learning Outcome:

We gained basic knowledge about aqua farming, its types, cultured species and its role in meeting the increasing demand for fish and other aquatic products.

02

Week 2: Pond Structure and Maintenance

During the second week, we studied the construction, structure and maintenance of fish ponds. We understood that proper pond preparation and water management are essential for healthy fish growth and successful fish production.

Topics Covered:

  • Different types of fish ponds used in aquaculture.
  • Selection of suitable land for pond construction.
  • Importance of pond depth, shape and water capacity.
  • Construction of pond bunds, inlets and outlets.
  • Removal of unwanted weeds and harmful organisms.
  • Importance of pond drying and preparation before stocking.
  • Water quality parameters such as temperature, pH and dissolved oxygen.

Practical Learning:

We observed pond structures and learned about water inlet and outlet systems. We also studied the importance of maintaining clean water, removing unwanted materials and checking pond conditions regularly.

Learning Outcome:

We understood how proper pond construction, preparation and regular maintenance improve the aquatic environment and help maintain healthy fish populations.

03

Week 3: Diseases, Feeds and Chemicals

During the third week, we learned about common fish diseases, nutritional requirements and different types of aqua feeds. We understood that proper nutrition and good water quality play important roles in maintaining fish health and growth.

Topics Covered:

  • Introduction to common fish diseases.
  • Causes of bacterial, fungal and parasitic infections.
  • Symptoms of unhealthy fish and unusual swimming behaviour.
  • Importance of protein, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins and minerals.
  • Different types of fish feed such as floating and sinking feed.
  • Ingredients used in fish feed, including fishmeal and soybean meal.
  • Importance of proper feed storage and quality.
  • Role of approved water-quality management products in aquaculture.

Practical Learning:

We observed different fish feed materials and learned about their nutritional importance. We also discussed how feed quality, feeding habits and water conditions can influence fish health and production.

Learning Outcome:

We gained knowledge about fish nutrition, feed ingredients, common diseases and the importance of responsible feed management in aquaculture.

04

Week 4: Identifying Fish Diseases

During the fourth week, we focused on identifying fish health problems by observing external symptoms and changes in fish behaviour. We learned that early identification of diseases helps prevent their spread and supports better fish health management.

Topics Covered:

  • Observation of physical changes in fish.
  • Identification of abnormal swimming and feeding behaviour.
  • Common external signs such as skin lesions and damaged fins.
  • Fungal infections and their visible characteristics.
  • Parasitic infections affecting fish health.
  • Eye abnormalities and changes in body appearance.
  • Swim bladder disorders and difficulties in maintaining balance.
  • Importance of regular fish health monitoring.

Practical Learning:

We observed fish health conditions and discussed how changes in body colour, movement, feeding response and physical appearance can indicate possible health problems. We also learned the importance of consulting fisheries experts for accurate disease diagnosis.

Learning Outcome:

We developed an understanding of fish health observation, common disease symptoms and the importance of early detection and professional guidance.

05

Week 5: Implementing Treatments and Water Management

During the fifth week, we learned about fish pond management practices and the importance of maintaining a healthy aquatic environment. We studied different methods used to manage water quality, reduce pollution and prevent the spread of fish diseases.

Topics Covered:

  • Importance of maintaining clean pond water.
  • Removal of excess organic waste and unwanted materials.
  • Monitoring dissolved oxygen, pH and water temperature.
  • Importance of proper aeration and water circulation.
  • Prevention of disease transmission between fish.
  • Basic biosecurity practices in fish farms.
  • Importance of using approved treatments under expert guidance.
  • Safe handling and storage of aquaculture inputs.

Practical Learning:

We observed pond cleaning and water management methods. We discussed how farmers monitor water quality and maintain suitable environmental conditions. We also learned that treatment decisions should be based on proper diagnosis and advice from qualified fisheries professionals.

Learning Outcome:

We understood the importance of pond hygiene, water quality management, disease prevention and responsible aquaculture practices.

06

Week 6: Growth, Feeding and Harvesting

During the sixth week, we studied fish growth, feeding management and harvesting techniques. We learned how farmers monitor fish development and maintain proper feeding schedules to achieve healthy growth and good production results.

Topics Covered:

  • Different stages of fish growth from fry to adult fish.
  • Importance of selecting healthy fish seed and fingerlings.
  • Regular monitoring of fish size and body weight.
  • Importance of balanced feed for fish growth.
  • Feeding schedules and proper feed management.
  • Different harvesting methods used in aquaculture.
  • Handling and transportation of harvested fish.
  • Introduction to freshwater prawn farming and its economic importance.
  • Post-harvest handling, icing and storage of fish.

Practical Learning:

We learned about fish growth monitoring and different harvesting methods. We also studied freshwater prawn farming and the importance of maintaining product quality after harvesting. Proper handling and cooling help preserve freshness during transportation and storage.

Learning Outcome:

We gained knowledge about fish growth, feeding practices, harvesting procedures and post-harvest management. We also understood how aquaculture contributes to food production, employment and economic development.

Overall Weekly Project Outcome

Through these six weeks of activities, we gained knowledge about aqua farming, pond construction, fish nutrition, disease identification, water management, harvesting and marine product preservation. These activities helped us understand the importance of sustainable aquaculture, community awareness and the economic value of aquatic resources.

Marine Product Processing

Methods used to preserve aquatic products

☀️

Sun Drying

Removing moisture from marine products through exposure to sunlight.

🧂

Salting

Using salt to draw out moisture and help preserve food.

❄️

Freezing

Lowering the temperature to preserve products and maintain quality.

🥫

Canning

Packing products in sealed containers using appropriate preservation methods.

Why is Processing Important?

Proper processing helps extend shelf life, maintain food quality, reduce waste and support food availability.
